A toilet that is overflowing or leaking at the base is an emergency, and our line for toilet repair in Coronado, CA answers 24/7. A toilet is a fixture that sends waste through a closet flange into the drain while a trap holds back sewer air. While you wait, shut the valve behind the bowl, stop flushing and lay towels around the base.

Most toilet calls trace back to a short list of causes. A fill valve that never shuts off, a flapper that leaks, a wax seal that has given up, a cracked bowl or a drain that is partly blocked. We find which one you have, tell you the price before work starts, then repair or replace the toilet. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

Toilet overflowing and flooding the bathroom: first steps

Water that keeps rising over the rim can soak the subfloor and reach the room below in a short time. Your goal before anyone arrives is to stop the inflow and keep water off flooring.

An after-hours overflow is handled the same way as a daytime one, so call at any hour and do these things while you wait.

  • !Lift the tank lid and press the flapper down into its seat, or hold the float up, so no more water enters the bowl.
  • !Turn the oval handle on the supply valve behind or beside the bowl clockwise until it stops.
  • !Do not flush again, and do not run the sink or tub that shares the same drain line.
  • !Lift rugs, set towels at the base and bail standing water out of the bowl with a bucket if the level is near the rim.
  • !If water is also rising in the tub or a floor drain, tell the dispatcher, because the blockage may sit further down the line.
  • !Keep cords away from wet floors and switch off the breaker if water reaches an outlet.

What toilet repair and installation actually involve

Most toilet repair happens inside the tank. A worn flapper lets water creep into the bowl, so the fill valve kicks on by itself, and a mineral-stuck fill valve can hiss for hours. Swapping the flapper, fill valve or flush valve brings back a normal flush using parts matched to your tank.

Repairs at the floor are a different job. A rocking bowl, a damp ring around the base or a sour smell often means the wax ring or the closet bolts have failed. The fix is to shut the water off, drain the tank and bowl, lift the toilet and set a new seal, then level and reconnect it. A broken flange gets repaired at the same time.

Installation covers a new toilet in place of an old one, or a first fixture in a remodeled bathroom. We check the rough-in distance, bowl shape and seat height your household needs, set the unit on a new seal, connect a fresh supply line and test several flushes. Ask the dispatcher about old-toilet removal when you book.

Signs a toilet needs a plumber

Some toilet problems can wait a day and some cannot. These are the signs that tell the two apart.

Water at the base

A damp floor after each flush points to a failed wax seal or loose bolts, not just condensation.

A bowl that rocks

Movement under your weight means the closet bolts, flange or floor beneath may be loose, and a leak is likely to follow.

Weak or slow flush

A partial blockage, a worn flush valve or a low tank water level can each cut flush power.

Constant refill noise

A tank that refills at odd hours has a leaking flapper or a worn fill valve.

Cracks in the china

A crack that seeps water cannot be sealed, so the bowl or tank has to be replaced.

A running or silently leaking toilet and the water bill

A toilet that keeps running after the flush can waste a lot of water without a single puddle. Cal Am's customer page notes that many leaks are not noticeable but still add to unexpected water use, and a quiet flapper leak fits that description. Drop a little food coloring in the tank and wait without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

A running toilet is worth a same-day call when available, because a worn fill valve can also fail outright and push water down the overflow tube nonstop. If the toilet checks out clean and the meter still moves, the loss is somewhere else, which is a job for leak detection rather than toilet parts.

When the toilet is fine but keeps backing up

Clogs that return every few days point at the drain beyond the bowl. A visit for drain cleaning can fix what a new toilet will not, and we say so up front instead of selling a replacement you do not need.

Gurgling when a nearby sink or tub drains is another clue that air is fighting through a partly blocked drain or vent. If more than one fixture is slow, the blockage is not in the toilet.

Coronado local note

Coronado permit note for removing and resetting a toilet

Coronado's Building Division publishes a permit handout (Handout 301, March 2025) that exempts removing and reinstalling a toilet, as long as no valves, pipes or fixtures are replaced or rearranged. That means a reset to fix a wax seal or to lay new flooring under the bowl does not by itself call for a permit.

Once the job replaces a valve or pipe or moves the fixture, it falls outside that exemption as the handout words it. Before you assume either way, a quick call to the city's Building Division settles it, since permit rules vary.

What decides the price of a toilet job

The price depends on what turns up once the toilet is opened, and you hear it before any work begins.

Repair or replacement

Tank parts cost far less than a new toilet, so the decision table above sets the baseline.

Model and parts

Older or unusual tank designs can need specific parts, and a standard unit is quicker to swap.

Flange and floor

A cracked flange or soft subfloor under a leaking toilet adds repair work beyond the seal.

Supply valve and line

An old shutoff that will not close often gets replaced during the visit along with a new line.

Access and timing

Tight bathrooms and urgent after-hours requests change the labor involved.

Before the plumber arrives

A few minutes of preparation speeds up the visit, whether it is a same-day appointment or a late-night call.

  • ✓Know where the supply valve sits and whether it turns, and tell the dispatcher if it is stuck.
  • ✓Note the brand on the tank lid or inside the bowl rim, which helps match parts.
  • ✓Clear a path to the bathroom and move rugs, hampers and cleaning supplies.
  • ✓Write down when the problem began and whether other drains are slow.
  • ✓Take a photo of any water on the floor or ceiling below for your records.
