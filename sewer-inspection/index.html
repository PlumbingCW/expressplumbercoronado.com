A sewer inspection in Coronado, CA sends a small waterproof camera through your sewer lateral so you can see the pipe condition without digging. It is a planned visit, the kind you book before buying a house, after repeated slow drains or before deciding on a repair. You see the footage as it runs and hear plain explanations of what it shows.

A camera answers questions that guessing cannot. Is that gurgle a clog or a crack? Are roots entering a joint? Does a section hold water? Knowing the answer first keeps you from paying for the wrong fix, and a clear price comes before work starts.

Coronado local note

What Coronado's permit handout means for sewer work

The City of Coronado Building Division's handout lists clearing stoppages and repairing leaks as exempt from a permit, so a camera inspection and cleaning visit usually proceeds without one. Replacing a defective concealed drain pipe is different, because it is treated as new work and needs a permit.

In practice that gives an inspection real value. If the footage shows damage, you will know early if the job is a leak fix or a replacement that needs permit planning. Rules can vary, so confirm with the Coronado Building Division before work.

How a camera inspection works

The plumber feeds a flexible rod with a camera head into the sewer cleanout, then pushes it along the lateral toward the city sewer main connection. A monitor shows the live picture and records it. The rod carries a transmitter, so the position of anything unusual can be found above ground.

Before the camera goes in, the line may need a quick cleaning if grease or debris would block the view. That adds a step, but a dirty pipe hides problems.

At the end you get a walk-through of the video and a plain explanation of any defects, with a clear price for any follow-up work before anyone starts it.

Good reasons to book a camera look

Most people call for one of these situations.

Slow drains that keep returning

A camera shows whether the cause is buildup, a root mass or a damaged section.

Gurgling toilets or sewer smell

These can point to a partial blockage or a vent problem, and the picture helps separate them.

Soggy or sunken ground

A wet patch along the pipe path can point to a leaking or broken lateral.

Before a repair quote

A recording lets you compare quotes against the same evidence.

Buying or selling a home

The lateral is hidden, and a recording documents its condition.

Cracked pipe on camera

A cracked pipe shows up as a dark line, a missing chunk or a joint that no longer lines up. Soil sometimes shows through, and water may stain the area around it. A hairline crack that does not leak is different from a break that lets dirt in.

Not every crack needs immediate repair. We describe each defect, its location and how serious it looks, then outline the options. If the footage shows a break, our sewer line repair page explains spot repair, lining and replacement.

Roots in the lateral

On camera, roots look like pale hairs or thick ropes pushing in at joints. They show where the pipe is already open and how much of the diameter they fill. A light fringe is a maintenance item, while a dense mass that blocks most of the pipe is urgent.

The footage guides the next step. A moderate root presence may be handled with cleaning, and heavier growth may call for hydro jetting after the camera confirms the pipe can take it.

An inspection when you are buying or selling a home

The sewer lateral runs underground, so a standard walk-through cannot show its condition. A camera inspection produces a recording of the line from the cleanout toward the street. Buyers can compare it against asking price, and sellers can address a defect before listing.

Coronado treats the lateral, from the house through the connection at the City main, as the owner's pipe. A defect found after closing is therefore the new owner's cost. Ask your agent or escrow officer what the sale contract requires, because we do not advise on contract terms.

What affects an inspection price

Pricing is stated up front, and it depends on a handful of things.

Access to the line

A proper cleanout makes the visit simpler than reaching the pipe through a toilet.

Length of the lateral

Longer runs take more time on camera.

Cleaning needed first

A line clogged with debris must be cleared so the camera can see.

Locating the defect

Marking a location above ground uses a transmitter and adds time.

Written or video report

A recording you can share with a buyer or contractor may be handled differently from a verbal walk-through.

Insurance seldom pays for an inspection by itself, though a claim for sudden damage may involve one. Ask your insurer, since we cannot promise coverage.
