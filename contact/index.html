What to have ready when you call

A minute of preparation helps the dispatcher assign the right plumber and gives you a more accurate picture of what to expect.

  • ✓Give the full street address, plus the unit number, gate code or building entrance if there is one.
  • ✓Describe what you see, hear or smell, such as water on the floor, a gurgling drain or no hot water.
  • ✓Say when the problem started and whether it is getting worse.
  • ✓Tell us whether you can reach the main water shutoff and whether you have already closed it.
  • ✓Mention anything that affects access, such as steep driveways, narrow streets, dogs or limited parking.
  • ✓Have a callback number ready in case the call drops.

Leaking, flooding or backing up: what to do while you wait

Help is dispatched as soon as you call, but there are a few things only you can do before the plumber arrives.

  • !Shut off the main water valve by turning it clockwise until it stops, then open a low faucet to drain the line.
  • !Stop using toilets, sinks and washing machines if a drain is backing up.
  • !Keep out of standing water near outlets, appliances and cords, and switch off a circuit only if you can reach the breaker on dry ground.
  • !Move furniture, rugs and valuables away from the wet area.
  • !If you smell gas, leave the building. From a safe place call 911 or SDG&E and do not use switches or phones inside.

Who to call besides us

Some problems belong to other agencies. For a gas smell, call 911 or SDG&E. For a problem on the utility side of a Coronado water meter, California American Water lists a 24/7 emergency line at 1-888-237-1333. For a sewer overflow in a Coronado street, the City of Coronado takes reports through its online work order system, and City Hall is at 619-522-7300.

For anything inside your walls, under your sink or in the pipe running from your building to the street, we can help. You can see everything we do on the services page, and if you want to compare plumbers, check any license at cslb.ca.gov before you hire.
