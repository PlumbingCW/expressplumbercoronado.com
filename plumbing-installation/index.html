Plumbing installation in Coronado, CA covers new supply lines, drains, fixtures, water heaters and gas piping added to a home or business. Installation means putting in new plumbing where none existed or replacing a system on purpose, as opposed to fixing something that broke. It runs on a schedule, and permit questions come up often.

Licensed and insured plumbers take on remodels, additions, new construction and appliance hookups. A clear price comes before work starts, and smaller jobs can sometimes get a same-day appointment when available. If you smell gas, skip this page and read the safety section first.

Smell gas? Leave the building first, then call from outside

A gas smell is not a plumbing appointment. It is a reason to get everyone out, then call for help from somewhere safe. Do not stay to look for the source.

Once you are out of the building, phone 911 or SDG&E from a safe spot. Keep away from light switches, indoor phones and anything that could throw a spark. We never promise a response time for a gas call.

  • !Walk everyone out, including pets, and leave doors open as you go if it is safe to do so.
  • !Do not flip switches, plug in devices, light matches or start a car in a closed garage.
  • !From a safe distance outside, call 911 or SDG&E.
  • !Do not re-enter until the utility or the fire department says it is safe.
  • !After the gas is shut off and the building is cleared, call us to repair or inspect the piping.

Gas line inspection and installation, safety first

A gas line carries fuel at low pressure through steel, flexible or coated pipe to a range, dryer, water heater or fireplace. A new appliance often needs a longer or larger line than the old one. Sizing matters, since an undersized line starves equipment at peak demand.

We inspect lines before an appliance is added, test joints for leaks with the proper equipment and install new runs with the correct fittings and shutoffs. Gas work is done by a licensed plumber, and any permit question goes to the Coronado Building Division. If an inspection finds a leak, the line is shut off until it is repaired.

Coronado local note

Which installs need a Coronado permit

Per the Coronado Building Division handout, a water heater install needs a permit, and that includes a tankless unit. If the gas line must be altered for the new unit, a licensed plumber has to supply gas line sizing calculations. Ask for the calculation in writing so the inspector has it on hand.

State law adds a seismic rule in Health and Safety Code section 19211, which says new and replacement water heaters must be strapped, braced or anchored against movement in an earthquake. Ask that bracing be part of any water heater install so it is not left out.

Anything else depends on the scope. Reach the Building Division by phone at (619) 522-7331 or by email at buildingpermits@coronado.ca.us before work starts, since rules differ by job. That handout also treats a defective concealed drain pipe replacement as new work, and it requires a permit for piping behind the wall face or below the floor.

Plumbing for a remodel or new build

New construction plumbing is done in stages. Underground or under-slab drains go in first, then supply and waste lines through the framing, and finally fixtures once finishes are done. Each stage is tested before it is closed, since a leak inside a wall or beneath a floor costs far more to fix later.

For remodels, the question is what the existing lines can handle. Adding a bathroom or moving a kitchen sink can overload a drain or require a new vent. If the old supply lines are failing, repiping may be worth discussing before the new fixtures go on.
