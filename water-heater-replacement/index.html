Flooding from a failed tank: the first ten minutes

A tank that has split at the seam or is pouring from the base is finished. A repair call cannot save it, so the goal now is limiting damage until a plumber arrives, whatever the hour.

  • !Close the cold water shutoff above the tank. If the handle is stuck, close the main water valve for the house instead.
  • !Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or turn a gas control to off. Do not step into standing water near live outlets.
  • !Open a hose bib or run the tub tap only if the plumber tells you to drain the tank. Otherwise leave it alone.
  • !Push water toward a floor drain with a squeegee or run a wet vac, and lift stored items onto shelves.
  • !Take photos of the damage and the unit's data plate before cleanup, since insurers and installers both ask for them.
Coronado local note

Permit first: what the Coronado Building Division handout says

The City of Coronado Community Development Building Division's permit handout (Handout 301, March 2025) states that installation of water heaters requires a permit. Swapping out a failed tank counts as an installation, and so does fitting a tankless unit. In practice, ask whoever quotes the job who is pulling the permit, and walk away from anyone who suggests skipping it.

The same handout notes that existing gas lines may need alteration, and that requires gas line sizing calculations by a licensed plumber. This comes up when a larger tank or a tankless unit will draw more gas than the old pipe was sized for, so tell dispatch the current fuel and model.

State law adds one more item. Health and Safety Code 19211 calls for a replacement heater to be secured against earthquake movement, so strap hardware is part of the install. Permit rules vary, so for an unusual layout phone the Building Division before work starts.

What to do while a failed tank waits

Turn off the power at the breaker, or set the gas control to off or pilot. Close the cold water valve at the top of the tank to stop more water from entering. Hot water can scald, so keep hands and children away from any leaking water.

If water is pooling, move belongings out of the way and use a wet vacuum on the floor. If you smell gas, do not use switches or phones inside. Leave the building, then call 911 or SDG&E.

No hot water after the failure: how to get warm water back fastest

Many households learn the tank is finished only when showers go cold. A like-for-like tank swap is usually the quickest road back to hot water, because the gas or power, the vent and the plumbing connections already exist in the right place.

A switch to a tankless unit or a heat pump is a good plan, but it is better scheduled than rushed. Those units can need new gas sizing, a different vent, a dedicated circuit or condensate drainage. If the family needs hot water tonight, say so on the call. Same-day and after-hours work is possible when crews are free, and a plumber can lay out the quickest safe option.

Signs the tank is past repair

Replacement is the sensible call when one or more of these shows up, even if the unit still makes some hot water.

Water pooling at the base

A puddle under a tank with dry fittings means the inner tank has failed. No patch holds for long.

Rust at every hot tap

Rusty hot water at all fixtures but clean cold water points to a corroding tank interior.

Cracking and rumbling

Loud noises from hardened sediment, paired with any dampness, suggest the base is stressed.

Repeat part failures

A second element, thermostat or thermocouple in a short time hints that the whole unit is wearing out.

Corroded seams or fittings

White crust or rust at the top and bottom connections often means the tank shell is next.

What happens on replacement day

1

Shut off and drain

The plumber isolates water and fuel, then drains the failed tank so it can be moved without a second flood.

2

Remove the old unit

The failed tank is disconnected and carried out. Ask about haul-away when you hear the price.

3

Set and connect the new one

The new unit is placed on a drain pan where the space allows, connected to water, gas or power, and vented as the manufacturer specifies.

4

Strap, valve and test

Bracing goes on, a relief valve and discharge line are set, and the unit is filled, fired and checked for leaks.

5

Close out the permit

Paperwork for the Coronado Building Division follows the install, and you get care notes for the new unit.

What decides the cost of a replacement

Replacement prices swing widely, so a number given before anyone has seen your utility space is a guess. The number comes after someone has seen your utility space, so none is printed here.

Type and size of the new unit

A 40 gallon electric tank and a tankless gas unit are very different jobs and very different purchases.

Gas or electrical changes

A larger burner may need gas line sizing and a new run. A heat pump may need a dedicated circuit.

Venting and location

Moving a unit or changing vent type adds labor and materials.

Code items

Bracing, a drain pan, a relief valve and discharge line, and sometimes an expansion tank belong in the price.

Removal and permit

Hauling the old tank and filing the permit are part of the work and should be itemised.
