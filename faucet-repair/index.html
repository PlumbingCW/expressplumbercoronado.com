Everyday faucet trouble is less dramatic. Drips, low flow, a handle that spins loose, a shower that will not hold its temperature, a tub that drains slowly. We repair the part when that makes sense and replace the fixture when it does not. Price comes first, and a same-day slot is booked when one is available.

Faucet, valve and fixture repair for kitchens, baths and laundry

A dripping spout nearly always comes from a worn cartridge, washer or seat inside the body. Taking the faucet apart shows which, and a matching cartridge usually costs less than a new faucet. Single-handle faucets use one cartridge, while older two-handle faucets have a stem on each side, so a hot-side drip means a hot-side repair.

Low flow is the other common complaint. Mineral crust clogs the aerator screen first, then the cartridge ports. Clearing both restores pressure, and if the body itself is pitted or the finish is peeling, replacement makes more sense than another repair.

Handles that wobble, spouts that leak at the base and sprayers that no longer retract fall into the same category. Each has a part that can be swapped, and the plumber will tell you if the faucet is past saving. Leaks under the sink from the trap or its connectors are fixed in the same visit.

Coronado local note

Cal Am hardness and the Building Division handout

The 2023 Cal Am water quality report gives the Coronado system's total hardness as a range from 145 to 280 ppm measured as calcium carbonate, which works out to 8.5 to 16.4 grains per gallon. The USGS scale calls that hard to very hard. Minerals at that level crust aerators and cartridge seats, so drips and weak streams return unless parts are cleaned or replaced. A vinegar soak helps in between visits.

If scale keeps coming back at every tap in the house, water softening and filtration is a separate conversation, since treating the supply is not a fixture repair. We will not push it to fix one faucet.

Under the City of Coronado Building Division handout, in-kind fixture replacement is allowed if the only parts swapped are exposed tail pieces, traps, trap arms and connectors. Piping behind the wall face or under the floor is treated differently and requires a permit. If your job goes past a like-for-like swap, check with the Coronado Building Division first.

What to do first when a faucet or valve fails

Close the shutoff valve under the fixture as soon as water is leaking where it should not. If the valve will not turn with a gentle hand, stop, because forcing an old valve can snap the stem. Close the main shutoff for the house instead and call.

Keep towels at the base of the cabinet, move anything electrical away from wet counters and floors, and take a photo of the leak and the fixture before cleanup. The photo helps a plumber identify the model and parts.

Broken or burst supply valve: stop the flooding first

A shutoff valve that snaps at the stem or sprays from a cracked body can empty a lot of water into a cabinet quickly. Pressure is steady, so the sooner it is stopped, the less drywall and cabinet you lose.

Call at any hour. A failed valve at 2 a.m. is still urgent, and daytime callers can often get a same-day visit when one is open. If the pipe itself has split rather than the valve, that is burst pipe repair.

Supply valves that stick, weep or will not close

Under-sink shutoffs turn rarely, and a valve that has not moved in years can seize, drip at the stem or fail the day you finally need it. Old multi-turn stops are the usual culprits, and a quarter-turn replacement is a common repair that also gets you a reliable shutoff for the next time.

Replacing a valve is best done on a calm day rather than during a flood. If a valve hisses, drips or will not turn, treat that as a warning and call before it breaks. A braided supply line that is kinked, rusty or bulging should be swapped in the same visit. Homes and shops in Chula Vista and other nearby towns can call the same number.

Fix the faucet or replace it

Parts are not the only factor. Age, finish and how often it has been repaired all feed the decision.

Repair makes sense when

  • The faucet is otherwise solid and only drips or runs low.
  • A matching cartridge, washer or aerator is available.
  • The finish is clean and the handle works smoothly.
  • You like the style and the last repair was a while ago.

Replacement makes sense when

  • The body is pitted, cracked or leaking at the base.
  • Parts for that model are hard to find.
  • The same faucet has been opened up more than once already.
  • You want a different style or a pull-down sprayer.

Showers and tubs that leak

Bath fixtures hide most of their parts behind a wall, so a small symptom can mean a bigger repair.

Shower valve drip

A showerhead that drips after shutoff usually has a worn cartridge in the valve behind the trim, and the trim plate comes off to reach it.

Showerhead leak

A leak at the arm or the head connection is often a worn washer or a thread seal that has dried out, a quick repair.

Tub spout that will not stop

A running tub faucet wastes water all night, so a same-day call is reasonable when available.

Shower swings hot and cold

A worn balancing cartridge or a mineral-stuck valve is the usual cause, and it is also a scald risk.

Drain clogged with hair

A slow shower or tub drain is usually hair and soap, and cleaning the trap or line fixes it. See drain cleaning for deeper blockages.

What affects the cost of a fixture repair

Fixture jobs are quick compared with pipe work, and you hear the price before any work begins.

Part or full fixture

A cartridge or washer is a smaller job than replacing a faucet, and the price follows the choice.

Finish and brand

Specialty finishes and imported models can mean harder-to-find parts and longer lead times.

Hidden access

Shower valves behind tile need careful access, and that adds time compared with a visible sink faucet.

Condition of valves

Seized shutoffs or old supply lines are often replaced during the visit for safety.

Timing

Urgent evening and weekend visits and same-day slots are scheduled around dispatch availability.

Policies often draw a line between sudden water damage and a part that simply wore out. Photograph the leak, read the policy wording and ask your insurer, since a claim outcome is not something we can forecast.
