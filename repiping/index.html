Repiping means replacing a home's water supply pipes, either all of them or a large section, with new pipe. Repiping in Coronado, CA is a planned project for most homeowners, driven by galvanized steel that has narrowed inside, copper that keeps springing leaks or water that comes out discolored. It is a bigger commitment than a single repair, so the choices below are worth reading before you book.

If a pipe is flooding something right now, that is an emergency and the answer is different. Close the main valve and call our 24/7 line. This page is for the quieter decision of whether to replace the plumbing, which material to pick, how permits work and how to compare the quotes you get.

Reasons homeowners decide to repipe

A single leak rarely justifies a whole-house repipe. These patterns are what usually tip the decision.

Repeated leaks in different places

When a second or third pinhole opens in separate parts of the system, the pipe itself is the problem, not any single joint.

Rust-colored or metallic water

Old galvanized steel can corrode inside, so water may run brown after sitting and then clear up.

Weak pressure on every fixture

Scale and corrosion shrink the inside of an old pipe, which cuts flow even when the street supply is normal.

A remodel that opens the walls

If framing or finishes are already coming off, replacing pipe at the same time costs less than doing it later.

Visible corrosion at joints and valves

Green crust, white scale or flaking metal at fittings often means the whole run is wearing, not just that spot.

PEX or copper: how the two compare

Both materials can make a sound repipe. The better choice depends on the house, the access and your priorities, and any good plumber will tell you the downsides of each.

Coronado local note

Coronado permits and the water inside your pipes

Coronado's permit handout leaves repiping off its exempt list, and it requires a permit for piping replaced behind the face of the wall or below the floor. Plan on one, leave time in the schedule and ask the plumber who pulls it. The Building Division can speak to your exact scope.

Water chemistry adds context about wear. Hardness in the treated water Cal Am delivers to Coronado runs from 145 to 280 ppm per the 2023 report, or 8.5 to 16.4 grains per gallon. Water that hard leaves mineral scale, and over many years that can narrow older pipes and clog fixtures. It does not prove any particular pipe is failing.

Galvanized pipe replacement

Galvanized steel is zinc coated iron pipe that was common in older construction. Over time the coating wears and the pipe rusts from the inside, building up a crust that narrows the opening. Pressure drops, water discolors and eventually the pipe leaks or splits. Because the damage is inside, the pipe can look fine from outside.

Replacement is usually all or nothing. Joining new pipe to old galvanized creates weak points, since different metals and old threads do not always get along. If you have a mix of galvanized and newer copper, a plumber can usually show you where each section runs and whether a partial replacement is realistic.

We do not state which Coronado homes have galvanized pipe, since it depends on the house. A plumber can check exposed sections at the water heater, the meter and under sinks to see what you have.

Whole house or partial: choosing the scope

A whole-house repipe replaces the supply lines from the main to every fixture. It is the most complete answer, and the walls may need several openings. A partial repipe replaces one branch, such as the kitchen and bathroom lines, and leaves the rest. It costs less up front, but the remaining old pipe can still fail.

If leaks have been isolated and the pipe elsewhere is sound, a repair is usually the better value, and our burst pipe repair page covers that path. If the pipe is failing everywhere, replacement is easier to justify than paying for repeated emergency visits.

Ask each plumber to list which fixtures and lines are included, what wall openings are expected and who handles patching. Quotes that look different often differ in scope, not price.

How a repipe proceeds from first call to final test

The sequence below is typical, and a good plumber walks you through it before work starts.

1

Inspect and scope

The plumber looks at the existing pipe, the water heater connections, the main valve and the access points, then lists what is to be replaced.

2

Permit and schedule

A permit application is prepared, and the work is scheduled around the inspection timeline and your household routine.

3

Water shutoff and rough in

The water is off for part of the day while new lines are run and connected, so plan for that.

4

Pressure test and inspection

New lines are pressure tested before walls are closed and are inspected as required by the permit.

5

Patch and restore

Drywall and paint patching is often separate from the plumbing work, so confirm who does it.

Copper pipe repiping and old copper that keeps leaking

Copper can last for decades, but in some conditions it develops pinholes or leaks at soldered joints. If your home has copper and the leaks are showing up in several places, a repipe with PEX or fresh copper may be the next step. If leaks are limited to one or two spots, a repair is usually enough.

A plumber may use a camera and pressure test to judge the state of the rest of the system before recommending replacement. For a hidden leak in a wall or floor, our leak detection service describes how the spot is found before anything is cut.

What decides the size of a repiping quote

A repipe is priced by scope, not by a flat rate. You hear the clear price before work starts, and the items below explain the range.

House size and fixture count

More bathrooms, kitchens and laundry connections mean more pipe and more connections.

Material choice

PEX and copper differ in material and labor, and the cheaper material is not always the cheaper job once access is counted.

Access and number of stories

A single-story home with an attic or open access is simpler than a two-story house with finished ceilings.

Wall and ceiling patching

The number and size of openings drives drywall, paint and sometimes tile repairs that may be billed separately.

Permit and inspection steps

The permit fee and inspection timing add to the schedule even when the pipe work itself goes quickly.

Insurers generally treat repiping from age or wear as maintenance rather than a covered loss. If leaks have caused sudden damage, that damage may be covered, so ask your insurer and keep records of every repair.
