Hydro jetting is the answer when a drain keeps clogging despite repeated snaking, or when a line has stopped completely and the cause looks like roots or heavy grease. In Coronado, CA the work is a controlled blast of high-pressure water through a hose that scours the inside of the pipe clean. Our line runs 24/7, and until help arrives you should stop using water and keep floor drains clear.

A jetter does what a cable cannot. A cable pokes a hole through a blockage, while a jetting nozzle sprays water backward and sideways against the wall of the pipe, cutting loose film, scale and root hairs. The pipe then returns close to its original width. We also tell you honestly when jetting is the wrong call, which matters as much as when it is right.

Coronado local note

Where your drain water goes after it leaves Coronado

The City of Coronado runs 17 sewer pump stations and more than 45 miles of underground sewer pipeline, and everything that goes down a drain ends up at the Point Loma Wastewater Treatment Plant. That is a long trip for a wet wipe or a slug of bacon grease.

The practical lesson is simple. Grease and wipes that settle in your own lateral cause the clog you have now, and anything that slips past your property can still collect at a pump station. Jetting cleans your line, but keeping grease and wipes out of the system is what stops the next call.

Jetting versus snaking, honestly compared

Neither tool is better in general. Each fits certain blockages, and a plumber who sells jetting for every clog is not giving you the full picture.

Snaking works best when

  • A single object or a soft mat of hair is blocking one fixture.
  • The pipe is otherwise clean and has not clogged before.
  • Access is limited and a small cable machine fits where a jetter does not.
  • The goal is a quick, lower-cost opening of one line.

Jetting works best when

  • Grease, scale or soap film coats the full wall of the pipe.
  • Roots have grown into joints and keep returning after cutting.
  • A line clogs again within weeks of a cable job.
  • A cleaner pipe is needed before a camera inspection or a lining job.

How a jetting visit works

The plumber feeds a hose with a specially shaped nozzle into the line through a cleanout. Water pressure drives the nozzle forward, and jets facing backward pull it along while it cleans. The operator slowly advances, then draws the hose back out, washing debris toward the opening or on to the main.

Most jobs start with a camera look so the plumber knows what is in the pipe before pressure goes on. That step protects you, because a jetter should never be aimed into a line that is already damaged or collapsed.

Afterward we run the camera again to confirm the pipe wall is clean and note anything that needs follow-up, such as a sagging section where water pools. You see the result instead of taking our word for it.

Tree roots in the line

Roots enter a pipe through joints and small cracks, drawn by the moisture and nutrients inside. Once in, they spread into a net that catches paper, grease and everything else. The usual symptom is a drain that slows, clears with a cable and slows again, sometimes with a gurgle in a nearby toilet.

A cable cutter can chop roots at the center of the pipe, but the cut stubs regrow and the outer mass stays in place. Jetting removes much more of the root growth and washes out the debris it held. It does not repair the opening the roots came through, so roots often return unless the pipe is fixed.

That is why we pair root jobs with a camera look. If a joint is open or the pipe is cracked, the lasting fix may be sewer line repair, and the jetting only buys time.

Repeat clogs after snaking

A line that needs cabling every few months is telling you something. Either the pipe wall has built up a layer of grease or scale, or roots keep growing back, or a belly in the pipe holds water where debris settles.

Another cable run only repeats the temporary fix. We recommend one thorough jetting pass followed by a camera check, then a plan to watch the line. If the camera shows a low spot, no cleaning will fully solve it, and we will discuss repair options instead.

For routine clogs in single fixtures, standard drain cleaning is usually still the right and cheaper choice. Jetting is for lines with a pattern.

Main line stopped or backing up: emergency steps

When the lowest drain in the house overflows and the cause may be a root-packed or grease-packed line, treat it as an emergency. Our dispatch is open 24/7 and we schedule same-day visits when we can, including nights and weekends.

  • !Stop all water use at once, including toilets, laundry and dishwasher.
  • !Do not pour chemical openers into a full line, because they sit in the pipe and endanger the person doing the work.
  • !Move items off the floor near the overflowing drain or tub.
  • !Find the sewer cleanout outside if you can and keep the area clear, since jetting usually starts there.
  • !Tell the dispatcher about earlier snaking, because that history helps choose the tool.

When jetting is not the right tool

High-pressure water is a poor fit for pipe that is cracked, collapsed, offset at a joint or badly deteriorated. Pressure can enlarge a crack, push soil into a break or drive water into a cavity around the pipe. Old, thin-walled or brittle pipe is the biggest concern.

We do not use a jetter on a line until a camera shows what the pipe can take. If the camera shows damage, we stop, explain what we see and quote a repair or a gentler cleaning method instead. Declining to jet is part of doing the job properly.

What decides a jetting quote

A clear price comes before any work begins. These are the main things that move it up or down.

Length and size of the line

A short run from a cleanout to the main takes less time than a long lateral with several bends.

Severity of buildup

Light film clears in one pass, while heavy scale or dense roots may need several passes with different nozzles.

Camera before and after

A camera adds time but confirms the pipe can take the pressure and shows the result.

Access point

A proper outdoor cleanout is easy to work from. An indoor-only access may need extra setup.

Time of the call

Late-night or weekend work can price differently, and the dispatcher tells you before you agree.

Home insurance rarely covers cleaning a line that clogged from buildup, though some policies respond to sudden damage. Ask your insurer, since we cannot predict a decision.
