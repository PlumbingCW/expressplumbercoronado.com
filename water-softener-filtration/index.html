Water softener and filtration questions in Coronado, CA usually start with white crust on fixtures, spotty glassware or a water heater that rumbles. A water softener swaps the calcium and magnesium that cause hardness for sodium or potassium, which stops scale from forming. Filtration is a different job, and mixing up the two is where most buyers go wrong.

Coronado local note

What Cal Am's Coronado numbers mean for a softener decision

Treated water in Cal Am's Coronado system comes from the City of San Diego's plants, and the 2023 report puts hardness between 8.5 and 16.4 grains per gallon. That sits in the hard to very hard bracket. For a household, it means scale is a constant and a softener is a reasonable tool, not a luxury.

The same report lists pH in the Coronado distribution system at 7.9 to 8.2, slightly alkaline. A softener does not change pH meaningfully, so it will not fix any taste or corrosion questions tied to it.

The smart move is to test your own tap, since water can change between a treatment plant and your faucet. Then ask Cal Am for its annual water quality report so you have current numbers in hand before choosing a unit size.

How a softener gets sized from grains per gallon

Sizing is arithmetic, not guesswork. The goal is a unit that regenerates every few days rather than every night or every month.

1

Find your hardness

Use a test strip, a lab test or the latest Cal Am report. Express the result in grains per gallon.

2

Estimate daily water use

Count people and fixtures. A household using 300 gallons a day is a reasonable example for a family of four.

3

Multiply for grains per day

Take 300 gallons at 12 grains per gallon. That is 3,600 grains to remove each day, or about 25,000 a week.

4

Match capacity to the gap

A 32,000 grain unit in that example regenerates roughly every eight or nine days. Undersizing means constant regeneration, which wastes salt and water.

Softener, conditioner, carbon filter or reverse osmosis

These products solve different problems, and the wrong choice is the most common source of disappointment.

ProductWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Salt-based softenerRemoves hardness minerals, so scale stops formingDoes not remove most contaminants, and adds a small amount of sodium or potassium
Salt-free conditionerChanges how scale forms so it clings less to surfacesDoes not remove hardness, so soap and spotting stay much the same
Carbon filterReduces chlorine taste and odor and some organic compoundsDoes not soften water or stop scale
Reverse osmosis at one tapTreats drinking water to a much finer levelDoes not protect the whole house, and wastes some water while it runs

What a softener does not do

A softener is not a drinking water purifier. It does not remove bacteria, lead, PFAS or most other contaminants, and it is not a substitute for a filter chosen against a lab test of your tap. Anyone who tells you otherwise is overselling.

Salt is the other trade-off. Salt-based units add sodium or potassium and discharge brine when they regenerate, so people on low-sodium diets should ask a doctor, and discharge rules can vary. Ask the Coronado Building Division if a permit is required for your install.

Water conditioner repair: when a unit stops softening

Most softener and conditioner faults come from a short list. A salt bridge or mushed salt in the brine tank stops the unit from making brine, a stuck bypass valve lets hard water through, and a clogged injector or a worn control valve prevents regeneration. Resin that has aged or been exposed to chlorine gradually loses capacity.

A plumber can check the bypass, brine line, drain line and control settings, then say whether a repair makes sense. Some control heads and resin tanks are not worth repairing, and you will hear that plainly. If the unit is also feeding a water heater, a flush of the heater may be due, covered by water heater repair.

Before you buy or install a unit

  • ✓Test your tap water or get the latest Cal Am water quality report, and write down the hardness in grains per gallon.
  • ✓Count the people in the home and estimate daily water use so the unit can be sized, not guessed.
  • ✓Decide whether you also want drinking water treatment, since that is a separate filter or reverse osmosis system.
  • ✓Pick a spot near the main water line with a drain, a power outlet and room to load salt.
  • ✓Ask about bypass valves, since a unit with no bypass is hard to service without shutting the house water off.
  • ✓Ask about the plan for the drain connection and any rules that apply before you commit.

What decides the cost of softening and filtration

Costs range from a single filter to a whole-house system, so a figure without the facts would mislead you. Pricing is laid out before the job begins, and this page skips dollar amounts because they would mislead.

System type and capacity

A small conditioner, a mid-size softener and a whole-house setup with a pre-filter differ in hardware cost.

Installation layout

A unit beside the main line with a nearby drain is a simple install. Long pipe runs raise labor.

Drain and power needs

A softener needs a drain for regeneration water and an outlet. Missing either adds work.

Salt or media supply

Salt, filter cartridges and resin replacement are ongoing costs after the install.

Repair versus replace

Old control heads and resin tanks may cost more to repair than a new unit saves.

Home insurance typically does not pay for a softener as an upgrade. Ask your insurer about water damage if a unit's connection ever fails, because we cannot promise coverage.
