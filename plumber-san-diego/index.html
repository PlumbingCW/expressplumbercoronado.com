Before we arrive: four quick moves for a San Diego property

In a downtown unit or a house, these four moves limit the damage while the plumber is on the way.

Stop the flow

Close the shutoff on the line coming into the building, usually near the meter or where the pipe enters. In a multi-unit building, ask the manager which valve serves your unit before turning off the whole structure.

Hold back on flushing

If a drain is overflowing or gurgling, skip toilets, showers and the washing machine until the line is cleared. Sending more water into a blocked lateral pushes sewage out of the lowest opening.

Respect the wiring

Do not step into standing water near outlets, appliances or extension cords. If you can reach the breaker without crossing a wet floor, switch off the affected circuit and wait for the plumber.

Make the door easy to find

Send the dispatcher your unit number, entry code and the best place to park or unload tools. Move belongings away from the wet area so the plumber can reach the fixture or cleanout straight away.

What owning the whole lateral means on a bad night

When sewage backs up into a tub at midnight, there is no City crew to dispatch to your property. The City will not dig on private ground, so the plumber you call handles the line from the building to the connection under the street. We start with a camera so you can see where the line is blocked, cracked or full of roots.

That camera work pays off in two ways. For an ordinary clog, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall and removes buildup that a cable alone can leave behind. For a collapsed or separated section, a sewer line repair replaces the broken run, and the video shows the owner and any property manager exactly what failed.

Owner's job or the City's: where the line is drawn

Policy gives the owner most of the responsibility, with one narrow exception outside the property line.

Usually the owner's responsibility

  • Any blockage inside the building or in the lateral before the street
  • Repeated maintenance clogs, with no limit on how many cleanings an owner must arrange
  • A blockage tied to missing routine maintenance, which can bring a bill for the City's response
  • Digging on private property, because City crews will not do it

Where the City may step in

  • A total blockage or break in the public right of way may be fixed at no cost to the owner
  • That outcome applies only if a plumber files a report first, and we cannot promise how the City will rule
  • Anything past the connection to the main under the street is the City's system

Coronado local note

From your drain to the Point Loma plant

Wastewater from the area ends up at the Point Loma Wastewater Treatment Plant after passing through your lateral and the City main. Because the first stretch of that path is your own pipe, what goes down your drain matters more than any treatment step downstream.

Grease, wipes and paper towels collect in the lateral first and build up there. Keeping them out of toilets and sinks is the cheapest way to avoid a bill for an overflow, and a regular camera inspection shows early whether roots or buildup are narrowing the pipe.

Hard water in the city supply: 145 to 280 ppm

San Diego's municipal water comes from City of San Diego Public Utilities. Cal Am's 2023 report on the treated water it buys from the Alvarado and Otay plants lists hardness of 145 to 280 ppm, which is 8.5 to 16.4 grains per gallon. That sits in the hard to very hard range.

In plain terms, expect scale on showerheads and inside heaters over time. If your tank is making popping sounds or running out early, a water heater replacement may be worth pricing once a technician confirms the tank is past saving.
