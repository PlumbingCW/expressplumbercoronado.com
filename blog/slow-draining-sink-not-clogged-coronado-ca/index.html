Why a Sink Drains Slowly When Nothing Seems to Be Clogged

A slow drain with no obvious blockage is usually a partial restriction, and where it sits depends on how many fixtures are affected.

By the Coronado Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026

Work down this ladder before you buy anything

A sink that drains slowly without a visible clog has a partial restriction somewhere between the strainer and the sewer. The fastest way to find where is to follow the drain downstream, one question at a time, and stop at the first step that changes the result.

1

Check the stopper and strainer

Pull the stopper, clean it, and look into the drain opening with a flashlight. Hair and soap film cling here more than anywhere else.

2

Empty and clean the trap

Place a bucket under the curved pipe, loosen the slip nuts by hand or with a wrench, and clear the trap. Reassemble and run the water.

3

Test other fixtures

Run the shower, the tub and the other sinks. If only this sink is slow, the restriction is local to its own pipe.

4

Listen for gurgling

Bubbling sounds or a toilet that burps when a sink drains point to a venting issue or a deeper restriction.

5

Note which fixtures are slow together

Several slow fixtures on the same floor suggest a branch line. Slow fixtures everywhere, especially the lowest ones, point toward the main drain or the lateral.

What each pattern usually points to

What you seeLikely causeReasonable next move

The hidden causes behind a slow sink

Grease and soap film

Kitchen grease and bathroom soap cool and stick to pipe walls. The opening narrows slowly, so the sink gets gradually slower, not suddenly stopped.

A partly blocked trap

The trap holds water on purpose, and it is also where debris collects. A trap that is partly full of sludge slows flow even when nothing seems stuck.

Poor venting

A drain pipe needs air behind the water. If the vent is blocked or missing, the water drains in slow, glugging surges.

Branch line buildup

The pipe that collects several fixtures collects scale, hair and residue over time. Every fixture feeding it slows down together.

A restricted lateral

The line from the house to the sewer main can narrow from buildup or roots. The first sign is often a sink that has gone sluggish for no clear reason.

What you can do versus what needs a camera and a professional

Reasonable at home

  • Cleaning the stopper, strainer and trap.
  • Using a plunger on a single slow sink with the overflow blocked.
  • Using a small hand auger on a fixture drain, if you stop when you meet resistance you cannot clear.
  • Running hot tap water for a minute after each sink use to move soft grease along.

Call a plumber for

  • More than one fixture slow at the same time.
  • Gurgling toilets or bubbles rising in a tub or shower drain.
  • A slow drain that returns within days of being cleared.
  • Any sewage smell, wet spot in the yard or backing up at the lowest fixture.

A word on chemical drain cleaners

Liquid drain openers can sit in a partially blocked pipe and damage fittings, and they are a hazard to whoever clears the drain next. If a bottle has already been used, tell the plumber, since the water in the trap may be caustic.

They also tend to hide the real problem. A cleaner might burn a hole through a soft blockage and bring a few days of better flow, while the grease film or branch buildup behind it stays in place.

When a camera inspection earns its place

If a drain has been cleared once and slows again within weeks, guessing stops being useful. A camera on a flexible cable shows whether the pipe has grease film, scale, a low spot that holds water, a joint that has shifted or roots entering at a seam.

That picture changes the plan. Film and scale call for cleaning or jetting. A sag or a broken joint calls for repair, and that is a different conversation about cost and, in some cases, permits. Our sewer inspection page explains what the camera records and how the footage is used.

What affects the cost of fixing a slow drain

Prices swing between a quick trap cleaning and a full line clearing. Pricing is stated before work starts and depends on which part of the system is involved.

Where the restriction sits

A fixture trap is quick to clear. A branch line takes more equipment, and a lateral takes the most.

Access

Cleanouts make the work simpler. When there is no cleanout, the plumber may have to go in through a vent or remove a fixture.

Method needed

A cable machine handles soft buildup, while hydro-jetting and camera inspection are used for heavy film or recurring trouble.

Whether it keeps returning

A drain that clogs every few weeks suggests a cause such as a belly or root intrusion, which needs inspection and sometimes repair.

Vent involvement

If a vent issue is found, the work may extend to the roof or to other parts of the system.

Routine drain clearing is maintenance and is not normally covered, but backup damage may be, depending on your policy, so ask your carrier if water has entered the home.

Habits that keep drains moving

  • ✓Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing so the fat goes in the trash, not the pipe.
  • ✓Fit a mesh strainer to every sink, tub and shower drain and empty it often.
  • ✓Flush a kitchen drain with hot water after washing greasy dishes.
  • ✓Do not wash coffee grounds, rice or eggshells down the drain.
  • ✓Remove and clean bathroom sink stoppers every month or so, before hair collects.
  • ✓Treat a first sign of sluggishness as a prompt to clean, not a problem to wait out.

Cleaning methods, from light to heavy

A plunger or hand auger handles a restriction close to the fixture. A cable machine reaches further, and our drain cleaning service page describes how the cable clears branch lines.

When the film is thick, scale has hardened or the problem keeps returning, hydro-jetting uses high pressure water to scour the pipe wall, and a camera shows what is left. If the camera points to the lateral, sewer line cleaning addresses the main run.

If you live just across the water

These diagnostic steps work in any house. A home in Chula Vista has sewer billing that depends on its water district, and in San Diego the city treats the whole lateral up to the main as the owner's responsibility. Check who handles what in your own city before deciding who pays for a main line fix.

Coronado local note

Coronado note: the lateral is yours, and clearing a stoppage needs no permit

Coronado's municipal code and the city's lateral page make the homeowner responsible for the sewer lateral up to and including the connection to the City sewer main. That has a practical meaning. If a slow drain traces to the lateral, the repair or cleaning is your responsibility, not the city's, even though part of the pipe is out in the street.

Coronado's Handout 301, published by the Building Division in March 2025, exempts clearing stoppages from a permit, along with repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures. A plumber can clear a slow drain without a permit being pulled. A defective concealed drain pipe that must be replaced counts as new work and does need one, so ask the Building Division at (619) 522-7331 if the camera shows pipe damage.
