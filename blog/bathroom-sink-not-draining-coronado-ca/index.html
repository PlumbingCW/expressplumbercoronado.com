Bathroom Sink Still Not Draining After Plunging? Causes and Fixes for Coronado, CA

When a plunger and a quick cleaning did not work, the blockage is usually in the stopper assembly, the trap, the vent or the branch line.

By the Coronado Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026

Why the plunger did not work

A bathroom sink that stays slow after plunging usually has one of four problems. The pop-up stopper mechanism is coated in hair and soap, the P-trap under the sink is packed, the vent is restricted so the drain cannot breathe, or the branch line behind the wall is partly blocked. Each has a different symptom, and the table below separates them.

A plunger works only when it can move water against a blockage close to the drain. If the clog sits deeper, or air cannot enter behind the water, plunging just shuffles the same water back and forth. Start with the cheapest, closest cause and work outward.

Clean the stopper assembly in about fifteen minutes

This is the fix that works more often than people expect. You need a bucket, a flashlight, an old toothbrush and a pair of gloves.

1

Clear the cabinet and set a bucket

Empty the space under the sink and place a bucket beneath the trap in case any water remains in it.

2

Free the pivot rod

Look behind the tailpipe for a horizontal rod held by a small nut. Unscrew the nut by hand or with pliers and slide the rod out of the pivot ball, which releases the stopper.

3

Lift the stopper out

Pull it straight up from the top of the sink. Most come out with a gentle tug and a trailing clump of hair and slime.

4

Scrub and probe

Wipe the stopper clean, then use the toothbrush and a straightened wire hanger with a small hook to pull debris from the drain throat.

5

Reassemble and test

Slide the rod back into the pivot ball, hand-tighten the nut, and run hot water. If it flows freely, you are done. If not, move on to the trap.

Opening the trap if the stopper was not the problem

1

Place the bucket and loosen the slip nuts

The curved P-trap is held by two slip nuts. Turn them counterclockwise by hand and catch the water that spills out.

2

Empty and inspect the trap

Dump it into the bucket. A heavy, gray sludge of hair, toothpaste and soap is the usual find.

3

Check the wall stub

Shine a light into the pipe that goes into the wall. A short length of flexible brush can reach the first few inches.

4

Reconnect carefully

Seat each washer straight, tighten the nuts by hand and a quarter turn more, then run water and check for drips.

Do it yourself or call a plumber

Reasonable to try at home

  • Removing and cleaning the pop-up stopper and pivot rod.
  • Emptying and cleaning the P-trap with a bucket underneath.
  • Using a plastic drain strip or small brush in the first few inches.
  • Cleaning the overflow channel and stopper housing.
  • Flushing with hot tap water after any cleaning.

Better left to a plumber

  • A clog that remains after the stopper and trap are clean.
  • Gurgling or slow drains at more than one fixture.
  • Water backing up into the tub or toilet when the sink drains.
  • Corroded or seized fittings that crumble when touched.
  • Any sewer smell that continues after the drain is open.

Why chemical drain openers are a poor bet

Caustic openers can sit in a standing sink and eat at fittings, and they can splash when you finally plunge. They also rarely cut through a hair-and-soap mat completely, so you end up with a partly open drain and a chemical-filled trap.

If you have already used one, tell the plumber. It changes how they handle the trap and what protective gear they wear.

When the problem sits further down the line

A blockage past the trap and into the branch line needs a cable, not a plunger. A small hand auger can reach some of these, but a pass with a powered cable on a drain cleaning visit clears soap, hair and scale from the pipe wall and tests whether other fixtures share the problem.

If a camera shows damaged or heavily scaled pipe, clearing alone will not hold for long. That is a conversation about plumbing installation work for new sections or fittings, and it should come with a clear price before work starts.

Keeping the bathroom sink open

  • ✓Pull and rinse the stopper every month or two, before the slow drain starts.
  • ✓Keep a drain screen in place to catch hair before it gets to the trap.
  • ✓Rinse the sink with hot water after shaving or brushing to flush soap.
  • ✓Wipe the overflow opening with a small brush so biofilm does not build up.
  • ✓Avoid pouring oils, wax or cosmetics wipes down the drain.
  • ✓Notice any gurgling early and write down which fixtures it affects.

What decides the price of a bathroom drain call

Most bathroom sink calls are small jobs, but a few factors move the price. The plumber quotes the price up front, before any tools come out.

Where the clog sits

A stopper or trap job takes minutes, while a branch line clog needs a cable and more time.

Access under the sink

Tight cabinets, seized nuts or a corroded trap add work and sometimes parts.

Single fixture or shared line

If the tub and toilet are affected, the plumber is clearing a larger section of pipe.

Parts that need replacing

A worn stopper, pivot rod or trap may be swapped for a matching part while the sink is open.

Repeat blockages

A drain that clogs again quickly can call for a camera look, which changes the scope of the visit.

Routine clogs are normally a maintenance matter and not an insured loss, though water damage from an overflow may be treated differently, so check your policy.

Coronado local note

Coronado note: permits and the soap scum in your sink

The City of Coronado Building Division's permit handout lists clearing stoppages as exempt from a permit, and it lists repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures the same way. So cleaning a stopper, opening a trap or running a cable does not need a permit. It also allows in-kind fixture replacement when only the exposed tail pieces, traps, trap arms and connectors are replaced.

That keeps the first steps simple. If the fix turns into something that moves or replaces concealed piping, permit rules vary, so ask the Coronado Building Division before work begins.

Water chemistry matters at the sink, too. The 2023 report from California American Water puts the hardness of Coronado's treated water at 145 to 280 ppm. Hard water leaves soap scum and mineral scale, in general terms, so you may need to clean the stopper and trap a bit more often than you expect.
