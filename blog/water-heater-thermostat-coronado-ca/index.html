Replacing an Electric Water Heater Thermostat Safely

An electric tank heater has two thermostats and two elements, and testing them in the right order tells you what actually failed.

By the Coronado Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026

Safety first: this is a power job, not a plumbing job

An electric water heater runs on household power at a level that can seriously hurt you. Before any cover comes off, switch the heater's breaker off at the panel, then confirm with a tester that no power reaches the wires. A breaker label can be wrong, and a tester is the only proof.

This guide covers electric tank heaters only. A gas water heater has a gas control valve, and that is never a homeowner repair. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and phone SDG&E or 911 once you are clear of the building.

How the thermostats decide whether you have hot water

Most electric tank heaters have an upper and a lower thermostat, each switching its own heating element. The upper one heats the top of the tank first, then hands off to the lower one. Above the upper thermostat there is usually a red reset button, the high-limit cutoff that trips if the water gets too hot.

A bad thermostat can give you no hot water, lukewarm water, water that runs hot and cold in cycles, or water so hot it is unsafe. Because elements fail with the same symptoms, you test before you replace.

Replacing a thermostat the careful way

Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for your model, because thermostat styles and wiring layouts differ.

1

Cut power and prove it

Turn off the breaker, remove the access panel and insulation, and use a non-contact voltage tester or meter on the terminals to confirm no power.

2

Photograph the wiring

Take clear pictures of every wire before disconnecting anything. Label the wires with tape if the manual suggests it.

3

Test the old thermostat and the element

With the wires off, use a multimeter as the manual directs. Test the element too, since a bad element can look like a bad thermostat.

4

Install an exact match

Use the same type and rating as the original. Make sure the thermostat sits flat against the tank wall, because it senses tank temperature by contact.

5

Close up, then restore power in order

Reconnect wires per your photos, replace insulation and covers, refill the tank fully if it was drained, and only then turn the breaker back on.

What a homeowner can handle and what to hand off

Reasonable for a careful DIYer

  • Checking the breaker and pressing the reset button once.
  • Lowering a thermostat setting if the water is too hot.
  • Replacing a thermostat with an exact match when the power is proven off and the wiring is photographed.
  • Draining a few gallons to look at the sediment that comes out.

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Any gas control valve, burner or pilot work on a gas heater.
  • Elements that will not come out, or a tank that leaks.
  • Repeated breaker trips or a reset button that trips again and again.
  • Scorched wires, melted insulation or any burning smell.

Why elements and thermostats tend to fail around the same time

Elements work by heating the water around them, and a coating of scale acts like a blanket that traps heat against the metal. The element runs hotter, wears faster and can trip the high-limit switch. That is why a thermostat swap sometimes fixes nothing, because the element was the part under strain.

If you do replace an element, use the manufacturer's element wrench and thread seal guidance, and drain the tank first. Never power the heater until the tank is completely full and you have let air out of a hot tap. A dry element can burn out in moments, and that damage is permanent.

Mistakes that turn a small repair into a big one

Trusting the breaker label

Panels are mislabeled more often than people think. Test the terminals before touching them.

Heating an empty tank

Elements burn out within minutes when they are not covered by water. Fill the tank before restoring power if you drained it.

Buying a non-matching part

Voltage, wattage and thermostat style must match the heater's label.

Loose thermostat contact

A thermostat that does not touch the tank wall reads the wrong temperature and can overheat the water.

Skipping the reset button check

A tripped high-limit can look like a dead heater. Pressing it once is free, but if it trips again, find out why.

What shapes the cost of a thermostat repair

The thermostat itself is a small part. Most of what you pay for is diagnosis, safe access and what else turns up.

Which parts failed

A single thermostat is quick. A thermostat plus a burnt element takes longer and may need draining.

Age and condition of the heater

On an older tank with a corroded base, spending on parts may not make sense compared with replacement.

Sediment and element access

Scale locked around elements can make them hard to remove and sometimes damages threads.

Wiring condition

Brittle or scorched wires need repair or replacement before a new thermostat can be trusted.

Code items nearby

A heater found without proper bracing or other required items may need those corrected as part of the work.

Insurance usually will not pay for a worn thermostat, but a sudden tank leak and the water damage it causes may be covered, so photograph the damage and ask your carrier.

  • ✓Switch off the breaker, then test the terminals with a tester you have confirmed works.
  • ✓Keep your phone nearby and a flashlight on, so you can photograph wiring without moving your hands near terminals.
  • ✓Make sure the floor is dry and you are not standing in water.

  • ✓Decide in advance that any burnt smell, melted plastic or tripping breaker means you stop and call.

Repair or replace: a quick way to decide

If the heater is newer, not leaking and only needs a thermostat or element, repair is almost always the better value. If the tank is leaking from the bottom or sides, no thermostat will help, and you are shopping for a new heater.

Coronado local note

Coronado note: bracing law and hard water sediment

Under Health and Safety Code section 19211, a home water heater of up to 120 gallons has to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, and so does every new or replacement unit. If you open the heater for a thermostat swap, look at the straps while you are there. A heater without them is out of step with state law, and a tank that shifts can tear its own wiring and pipes.

Hardness is the second local issue. Cal Am's 2023 water quality report for its Coronado system gives a range of 8.5 to 16.4 grains per gallon, from 145 to 280 ppm. Water that hard leaves scale on the bottom of the tank and around the elements. That is why a heater here can have sediment trouble that looks like a thermostat problem, and why a short drain to check the sediment is a useful part of any diagnosis.
